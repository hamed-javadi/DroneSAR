3D Radar Imaging from the UAV Nadir

An interferometric synthetic aperture radar (InSAR) framework for reconstructing 3D imagery directly beneath a drone using a lightweight 60 GHz MIMO mm-wave radar with phase gradient autofocus.

S. Hamed Javadi1 • Hichem Sahli1,2 • André Bourdoux1 (Senior Member, IEEE)
1Interuniversity Micro-Electronics Center (IMEC), Leuven, Belgium • 2Vrije Universiteit Brussel (VUB), Brussels, Belgium
Executive Overview

Abstract

Radars improve the sensing robustness of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) by operating under poor lighting and weather conditions and seeing through occlusions such as vegetation. However, they suffer from poor angular resolution, which can be addressed using synthetic aperture radar (SAR) algorithms. State-of-the-art UAV SAR methods operate at a depression angle and are not suitable for sensor fusion applications where the data are collected from areas directly below the UAV (i.e., the UAV nadir).

In this paper, we present an interferometric SAR (InSAR) framework for reconstructing 3D images from the UAV nadir using a low-cost multi-input-multi-output (MIMO) mm-wave radar. Additionally, an effective method based on the phase gradient autofocus (PGA) is presented for compensating the phase error across the virtual receive antennas. We extend the framework to account for the UAV attitude angles and evaluate the proposed pipeline in both extensive simulations and real-world flight experiments over complex terrain.

Conventional Depression-Angle SAR
  • Looks sideways at 20°–70° depression angles, creating a blind spot directly beneath the drone (nadir).
  • Disconnect with downward optical, multispectral, and LiDAR sensors used on standard UAV payloads.
  • Requires a significantly larger maximum unambiguous range \(r_{\text{max}}\), limiting the flight altitude ceiling.
Proposed Downward Nadir InSAR
  • Reconstructs digital elevation maps (DEM) and 3D point clouds of the ground directly beneath the UAV.
  • Zero grazing angle geometry (90° incidence) minimizes layover and foreshortening distortions.
  • Seamless multimodal fusion with nadir cameras for all-weather search and rescue, agriculture, and robotics.
Core Scientific Framework

The UAV Nadir InSAR Reconstruction Pipeline

A step-by-step walkthrough of the mathematical algorithms transforming raw mm-wave FMCW radar beat signals into calibrated, autofocused 3D point clouds from the UAV nadir.

Fig. 3: The Complete InSAR Framework (Click image to zoom): Motion compensation, Polar-to-grid interpolation, Fast-time IFT, Phase error correction via PGA \(e^{-j\epsilon(n)}\), Slow-time IFT, Rayleigh segmentation (RaySe), and Interferometric processing across virtual antenna pairs.

1
MIMO Transceiver & Nadir Geometry
Geometry & Signal Model
The antenna array is installed on the drone's underside pointing directly downward (zero grazing angle), with the baseline along the across-track (\(z\)) direction. The transmitted FMCW chirp is modeled as:
$$s_T(t) = a_c \exp\left[ j\left(\omega_c + \frac{\gamma}{2}t\right)t \right]$$
Input: FMCW Chirp Cube Output: Beat Signal \(s(i, n)\)
2
Coherent Beamforming & PFA
Spatial Interpolation & SNR
Beamforming (BF) across the MIMO elements coherently integrates signals w.r.t. the SAR Reference Point (SRP) at nadir, boosting the SNR. Polar Format Algorithm (PFA) then interpolates wavenumber samples onto a Cartesian grid \(k_x[n'], k_y[i']\):
$$s_C(i', n') \xrightarrow{\text{2D-IFT}} \sigma(x_s, y_s)$$
Input: Raw Array Echoes Output: Grid-Mapped \(s_C(i', n')\)
3
Phase Gradient Autofocus (PGA)
Motion Compensation
Along-track velocity perturbations \(v_e\) induce dominant quadratic phase errors in slow time: \(k_x(i, n)\epsilon(n) \approx k\alpha' v_e T_c n^2\). PGA estimates the phase error gradient across dominant scatterers, compensating each receive channel by:
$$\exp[-j\epsilon(n)] \quad \text{with} \quad \epsilon(n) = \int \dot{\phi}(n) dn$$
Input: Defocused Range Profile Output: Phase-Compensated SAR
4
Rayleigh Segmentation (RaySe)
Scatterer Isolation
SAR background speckle noise amplitude follows a Rayleigh distribution. RaySe estimates the Rayleigh scale parameter \(\hat{\sigma}_R = \sqrt{\frac{\text{Var}(A)}{2 - \pi/2}}\) and sets an upper-tail threshold to isolate prominent scatterer coordinates \((x_s, y_s)\):
$$\tau = \hat{\sigma}_R \sqrt{-2\ln(P_{\text{fa}})}$$
Input: SAR Images \(S_{R_1}, S_{R_2}\) Output: Detected Major Peaks \((x_s, y_s)\)
5
Multi-Baseline Phase Unwrapping
Ambiguity Resolution
Interferometric phase difference \(\phi = \angle(S_{R_1} \odot S^*_{R_2})\) is wrapped in \((-\pi, \pi]\). Two antenna pairs with coprime baselines (\(B = 7\frac{\lambda_n}{2}, B' = 5\frac{\lambda_n}{2}\)) resolve integer ambiguity numbers \((k, k')\) without clustering:
$$k, k' = \arg\min_{k,k' \in \mathbb{Z}} \left| \frac{B}{B'}k' - k + \frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{B}{B'}\phi' - \phi\right) \right|$$
Input: Wrapped \(\phi, \phi'\) Output: Unwrapped Phase \(\psi = \phi + 2\pi k\)
6
Roll-Aware Across-Track Estimation
3D Point Cloud Formation
Accounting for UAV roll attitude \(\rho\), the analytical across-track coordinate \(z_s\) is computed. Furthermore, the misregistration limit \(\theta' < \frac{\lambda}{B(N_c T_c)} \approx 624^\circ/\text{s}\) guarantees that normal drone flights (~8°/s) require zero image registration resampling:
$$z_s = R_y \frac{\sin\rho\cos\rho \pm \sqrt{\kappa^2\sin^2\rho + \cos^2\rho}}{\kappa^2 - \cos^2\rho}$$
Input: Unwrapped \(\psi\), Depth \(R_y\) Output: 3D Point Cloud \((x_s, y_s, z_s)\)
